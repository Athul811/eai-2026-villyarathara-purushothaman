## The hard parts

These are the parts that people find difficult. Read them before the
session. If you cannot follow one of them, make it your question.

- **BPMN is a specification, and not a picture.** Each element has a defined behaviour. A diagram that looks correct can specify the wrong process, and the difference is visible only if you know the notation.
- **A saga has no isolation.** Between the first step and the last step, another process can read the intermediate state. A database transaction hides that state. A saga cannot, and a semantic lock is the usual answer.
- **The engine removes your retry code, and it adds a cluster.** You no longer write the retry loop, the timer or the state store. You now operate a server, and you must monitor it.

## Self-check

Answer these before the session. They are not graded, and I do not
collect them. They show you which of the hard parts you cannot yet
explain. **A question that you cannot answer is your question for the
session.**

1. Why can one database transaction not hold a process that calls a payment provider and a courier?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   The external systems are not in your transaction. They commit their own work, and you cannot roll them back. A long-running process also holds locks for too long.

   </details>

2. Your saga completes three steps. Another process reads the order between step two and step three. What does it see?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   It sees the intermediate state, because a saga has no isolation. The system took the payment. It did not yet reserve the stock. A semantic lock, such as a pending status, is the usual answer.

   </details>

3. Name three responsibilities that a durable execution engine takes from your code.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   It keeps the state of each workflow. It retries a failed activity by a policy. It continues the workflow after a worker crash. It also holds the timers.

   </details>

## What the assignment will require

**PA7 — port the saga to Temporal**

You keep the behaviour of your PA6 saga. You give the durability to an engine.
The graded demonstration: stop the worker in the middle of a saga, then start it again. The workflow continues and completes.
The assignment assumes that you can do these things:
- Start a seven-container stack with Docker Compose.
- Separate a workflow from an activity.
- Set a retry policy on an activity.
- Decide which failures an activity may retry.
- Compensate in reverse order inside a workflow.
- Name, in your ADR, the code that no longer exists.
